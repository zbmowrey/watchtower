# Positioning & ICP Definition

Use this before copy, pricing, keyword research, paid ads, outbound, or product-roadmap growth work.

## Do

- Choose one lead segment.
- Name the main alternative, including "do nothing" and spreadsheets.
- Define the category customers should place the product in.
- List differentiated capabilities.
- Translate capabilities into value.
- Select proof that supports the value claim.
- Write the one-page positioning canvas.
- Hand the canvas to copy, SEO, sales, onboarding, and pricing work.

## When

- Run for every new product.
- Rerun before a relaunch.
- Rerun when conversion falls with clean traffic.
- Rerun when sales calls require repeated re-explanation.
- Rerun when a new segment begins converting better than the current ICP.

## How

1. List current users, buyers, lost deals, and likely segments.
2. For each segment, score pain, urgency, reachability, willingness to pay, product fit, and expansion.
3. Pick the highest-scoring reachable segment.
4. Inventory alternatives the segment uses today.
5. Identify what the product does that alternatives cannot.
6. Map each capability to a business outcome.
7. Choose the category that makes the value easiest to understand.

## Watch

- Customers describing the product differently from the page.
- High-intent traffic that does not convert.
- Demo calls stuck on "what is this?"
- Segments with higher activation or retention than the named ICP.
- Competitor pages reframing the category.

## Avoid

- Positioning for all SMBs, all teams, or all users.
- Treating a feature list as positioning.
- Ignoring "do nothing."
- Writing headlines before choosing the segment.
- Changing channel strategy without updating positioning.
